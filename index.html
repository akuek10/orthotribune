Biomechanical pullout ranges represent mean failure loads documented across peer-reviewed cadaveric and foam models. Bubble size reflects estimated relative market utilization.

Clinical Caveat on Biomechanics: High static ultimate pullout strength in rigid bone does not imply superior clinical healing. In rotator cuff repair, most construct failures occur via suture-tendon cut-through under cyclic load or biological non-healing, rather than anchor pullout.

Biomechanical Takeaway: In Glenoid rim fixation, modern small-diameter (1.4–1.8 mm) all-suture anchors achieve pullout strengths (~240–310 N) comparable to traditional solid 2.8–3.5 mm screws, comfortably exceeding the cadaveric capsulolabral failure benchmark (~180 N) while conserving critical glenoid rim bone stock.
Material Selection Shifts: Illustrates broad qualitative paradigm shifts. The near-total decline of 1st-generation pure PLLA was driven by documented osteolysis and sterile cyst formation, yielding dominance to osteoconductive biocomposites and bone-sparing all-suture designs.

Rotator Cuff Augmentation & Graft Efficacy (2016–2026)

Longitudinal trends evaluating the lowering of clinical thresholds (ROHI / fatty infiltration triggers) and comparative structural re-tear rates across Acellular Dermal Matrices, Synthetics, and Xenografts.

Key Indication Trend: Clinical practice has transitioned from viewing patches strictly as salvage for massive, retracted tears (dropping from 68% down to <18% of patch usage) toward proactive onlay reinforcement in moderate/high-risk tears to safeguard the repair construct.
Efficacy Takeaway: Human Acellular Dermal Matrices (hADM) hold ~56% market share with structural re-tear rates of ~11.5%, compared to ~38% baseline failure in un-augmented high-risk controls. Early 1st-generation porcine xenografts have been largely abandoned due to aseptic synovitis and ~42% failure rates.

Shoulder Anchor Commercial Economics & Structural Evidence

Shoulder Suture Anchor Economics: Commercial Cost vs. Structural Integrity

A discrete comparative evaluation of facility acquisition prices (commercial wholesale ASP in USD) against published structural failure rates across randomized trials and multi-center cohorts.

Bankart Fixation: Anchor Number vs. Material

Recurrence dropped from ~18.5% to ≤10% when clinical practice shifted from under-dosing (<3 anchors) to placing ≥3 points of fixation. In comparative trials (Barber, Willemot), all-suture anchors show clinical equivalency / non-inferiority to solid PEEK; their adoption is driven by glenoid rim bone stock conservation rather than superior fixation strength.

Rotator Cuff: Structural Healing vs. PROMs

Transosseous-equivalent double-row constructs significantly improve structural tendon integrity (MRI healing) in large tears (>3 cm) over single-row repairs. However, meta-analyses (Duquin, Hein) show that when tendons heal, patient-reported outcome scores (ASES, Constant) are often similar. Furthermore, mechanical constructs alone cannot prevent re-tears in patients with advanced fatty degeneration (ROHI high-risk).
