Shoulder Suture Anchor Health Economics & Value Realization (2010–2026)

Evaluating the relationship between increasing index implant construct costs and long-term clinical value: attenuation of revision surgeries, improved ASES functional scores, and shortened return-to-activity timelines.

Cost-Effectiveness Frontier: Bubble size corresponds to mean ASES scores. While index construct prices increased from ~$500 to ~$1,300–$2,000, secondary revision burdens fell by >60% across both pathologies. Because a revision surgery costs $18,000–$28,000+ per episode, modern multi-anchor and knotless constructs fall well below standard incremental cost-effectiveness thresholds ($50,000/QALY).
Patient-Reported Outcomes (PROMs) & Rehabilitation: Transosseous-equivalent double-row constructs increased baseline ASES scores from ~79 to ~92 points and decreased time to baseline return-to-work/activity by over 8 weeks compared to single-row repairs, primarily driven by immediate construct stability and reduced immobilization requirements.
