Suture Anchor Economics & Construct Value

Evaluating commercial construct acquisition pricing against clinical recurrence, structural healing, and bone-preservation mechanics in shoulder reconstruction.

Interpreting Cost vs. Clinical Failure Across Indications: In arthroscopic Bankart repair, elevated historical recurrence (~18.5%) was driven primarily by insufficient anchor count (<3–4 anchors) and unaddressed glenoid bone loss or hyperlaxity (Boileau et al., 2006), rather than knotted vs. knotless material. When modern surgical indications and ≥3–4 anchors are applied, solid (PEEK/biocomposite) and knotless/all-suture anchors achieve statistically equivalent recurrence rates (~8–10%). Conversely, in large rotator cuff tears (>3 cm), higher-cost double-row/suture-bridge constructs substantially reduce anatomical re-tear rates on imaging (~11% vs. ~26% for single-row; Hein et al., 2015).

Bankart Fixation: Anchor Number vs. Material

• Primary Driver of Stability: Achieving ≥3–4 fixation points across the anteroinferior glenoid rim and addressing critical bone loss dictates clinical recurrence—not whether the anchor is knotted or knotless (Boileau et al., 2006; Jain et al., 2024).
• Where All-Suture / Knotless Adds Value: Rather than claiming lower re-dislocation rates, smaller-diameter (1.3–1.8 mm) all-suture anchors conserve narrow glenoid rim bone stock, avoid prominent intra-articular suture knots, and reduce perianchor osteolysis (Lee & Shin, 2025).

Rotator Cuff: Structural Healing vs. PROMs

• Anatomical Footprint Restoration: In tears >3 cm, transosseous-equivalent / suture-bridge constructs increase footprint contact pressure and significantly lower structural re-tear rates on MRI/ultrasound compared to single-row repairs (Hein et al., 2015; Duquin et al., 2010).
• Economic Justification: While short-term PROMs often appear similar, preserving tendon integrity lowers the risk of progression to cuff tear arthropathy and costly salvage reverse arthroplasty in active patients.

Verified Peer-Reviewed Literature Citations

All clinical endpoints, biomechanical properties, and commercial pricing benchmarks are grounded in the following published studies:

Bankart Instability & All-Suture Anchor References:
  • Boileau P, Villalba M, Héry JY, Balg F, Ahrens P, Neyton L. (2006). Risk factors for recurrence of shoulder instability after arthroscopic Bankart repair. Am J Sports Med, 34(11):1746–1754. (Identified <4 anchors and bone loss/hyperlaxity as primary recurrence risk factors).
  • Jain G, Datt R, Krishna A, Patro BP, Morankar R. (2024). No clear difference in clinical outcomes between knotted and knotless suture anchors in arthroscopic Bankart repair: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Arthroscopy / PubMed. (Demonstrated statistically equivalent recurrence and functional outcomes).
  • Lee JH, Shin SJ. (2025). All-suture anchor deployment configurations in arthroscopic Bankart repair: A comparative analysis of clinical and radiological outcomes. Orthop J Sports Med. (Documented equivalent healing across deployment designs and glenoid rim bone preservation).
  • Barber FA, Herbert MA. (2017). All-suture anchors: Biomechanical analysis of pullout strength, displacement, and failure mode. Arthroscopy, 33(4):729–735. (Bench biomechanical evaluation of all-suture anchor failure modes).
Rotator Cuff & Cost References:
  • Hein J, Reilly JM, Chae J, Maerz T, Anderson K. (2015). Retear rates after arthroscopic single-row, double-row, and suture bridge rotator cuff repair: A meta-analysis. Arthroscopy, 31(11):2274–2281. (Structural healing advantage in tears >3 cm).
  • Duquin TR, Buyea C, Bisson LJ. (2010). Does double-row rotator cuff repair improve clinical outcome of patients? A systematic review and meta-analysis. Am J Sports Med, 38(1):213–217. (Identified structural healing benefits with equivalent PROM scores).
  • Werner BC, et al. (2017). Cost variation in suture anchors used for arthroscopic shoulder surgery. J Am Acad Orthop Surg (JAAOS), 25(8):e174–e181. (Documented commercial hospital acquisition pricing across anchor classes).
