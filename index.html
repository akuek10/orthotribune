How-to: Interactive Charts

Biomechanical pullout ranges represent mean failure loads documented across peer-reviewed cadaveric and foam models. Bubble size reflects estimated relative market utilization.

Clinical Caveat on Biomechanics: High static ultimate pullout strength in rigid bone does not imply superior clinical healing. In rotator cuff repair, most construct failures occur via suture-tendon cut-through under cyclic load or biological non-healing, rather than anchor pullout.

Biomechanical Takeaway: In Glenoid rim fixation, modern small-diameter (1.4–1.8 mm) all-suture anchors achieve pullout strengths (~240–310 N) comparable to traditional solid 2.8–3.5 mm screws, comfortably exceeding the cadaveric capsulolabral failure benchmark (~180 N) while conserving critical glenoid rim bone stock.
Material Selection Shifts: Illustrates broad qualitative paradigm shifts. The near-total decline of 1st-generation pure PLLA was driven by documented osteolysis and sterile cyst formation, yielding dominance to osteoconductive biocomposites and bone-sparing all-suture designs.

Biomechanical & Clinical Literature References

Derived directly from published cadaveric testing protocols and clinical complication cohorts:

Biomechanical Failure Benchmarks:
  • Barber FA, et al. Biomechanical testing of modern suture anchors in the glenoid and greater tuberosity. Arthroscopy. (Established comparable load-to-failure between 1.4 mm soft anchors and solid PEEK in cancellous bone).
  • McMahon PJ, et al. Tensile strength and failure load of the native anterior-inferior glenoid labrum. AJSM. (Documented native labral failure load range of ~150–220 N).
  • Mazzocca AD, et al. Biomechanical evaluation of double-row and transosseous-equivalent rotator cuff repairs. Am J Sports Med.
Material Evolution & Complications:
  • Pape D, et al. Osteolysis and sterile cyst formation associated with pure PLLA bioabsorbable suture anchors. JSES. (Documented clinical evidence leading to the clinical phase-out of 1st-gen pure PLA).
  • Pflueger J, et al. Metal anchor dislodgement and third-body chondral damage in glenohumeral instability repair. Arthroscopy. (Clinical rationale for metal abandonment on the glenoid rim).
