Clinical Positioning: Rather than asserting cross-platform PROM superiority in the absence of head-to-head RCTs, the evidence supports a functional division of labor: Human Dermal Allograft (hADM) provides structural load-sharing and bridge-augmentation in large-to-massive or revision repairs with compromised tissue stock, while Bio-inductive Bovine Collagen (REGENETEN) stimulates induction of native-like host tissue thickness in partial-thickness or lower-tension medium tears.
Structural Healing vs. Historical Controls: Multiple independent systematic reviews and RCTs demonstrate that both acellular human dermal matrices (~10%–15% retear) and bio-inductive collagen implants (~8%–12% retear) significantly reduce structural failure compared to unaugmented historical controls (~35%–45% in large/massive tears). Both modalities improve structural integrity over baseline repair alone.
Clinical Mechanism & Indication Fit: The economic value of a graft is maximized when matched to the appropriate biomechanical challenge. hADM offers high tensile strength, load-sharing, and tissue interposition when native tendon quality is degraded (Goutallier ≥2, revision RCR, high gap formation risk). In contrast, bio-inductive collagen serves as an inductive scaffold to promote biological healing and tendon remodeling without mechanical tension-bearing.
Structural Failure Does Not Equal Surgical Revision: A foundational tenet in rotator cuff health economics: only a minority of structural MRI failures proceed to secondary surgical intervention. Many patients with anatomic failure remain clinically functional and asymptomatic. Modeling revision costs based solely on MRI retear rates leads to inflated economic projections.
Selective Augmentation Economics: In published decision-analytic Markov models (Nwachukwu et al., Makhni et al.), routine graft deployment across all unselected small tears is not cost-effective. However, in select high-risk cohorts (massive tears, revisions, diabetes, smokers), avoiding symptomatic re-tears and downstream salvage yields an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) well below standard willingness-to-pay thresholds (<$50,000/QALY).

Human Dermal Allografts (hADM)

• Primary Role: Structural reinforcement and tension-sharing.
• Ideal Cohort: Large-to-massive tears (>3 cm), revision repairs, poor tissue quality, high retraction.
• Value Proposition: Protects the repair construct mechanically during the vulnerable 0–12 week healing window.

Bio-inductive Collagen Implants

• Primary Role: Biological induction and tendon thickness restoration.
• Ideal Cohort: High-grade partial tears, primary medium tears without severe retraction, intact footprint purchase.
• Value Proposition: Induces new host collagen tissue without requiring extensive biomechanical fixation.

Value Analysis Committee (VAC) Takeaways

• Avoid Universal Mandates: Augmentation should be restricted to high-risk clinical indications.
• Total Episode Evaluation: Reducing symptomatic revision episodes in high-risk patients offsets the initial graft acquisition spend.
• Realistic Metric Framing: Focus on durable structural retention and avoiding revision, rather than claiming cross-platform PROM superiority.
