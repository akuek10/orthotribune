Risk-Stratified Health Economics: ROHI Selective Augmentation

An evidence-based health economic evaluation modeling clinical failure ranges, a fixed 30% surgical revision conversion rate, and sensitivity analysis across international and US revision cost thresholds ($15,000–$60,000 USD).

Low Risk (ROHI 0–4 Points)

Consistently Value-Negative.
Because baseline failure is already low (6–10%), the absolute risk reduction is only ~1.5% (yielding 0.45% avoided surgical revisions). Avoided revision savings cannot offset the $2,800 USD graft invoice within any clinical cost ceiling (Breakeven requires >$600k revision cost).

Moderate Risk (ROHI 5–8 Points)

Negative in Standard Episode Costs.
Baseline failure is 18–25% (dropping to 10–15% augmented, ARR: 9.0%), yielding 2.7% avoided re-operations. Under standard healthcare costs ($25,000–$35,000 revision), net value remains negative (-$2,125 to -$1,855 USD). Breakeven occurs only if revision episode burden exceeds $103,704 USD.

High Risk (ROHI 9–15 Points)

Crosses Breakeven at ~$42,424 USD.
Baseline failure is high (35–45%). Augmentation drops failure to 14–22% (ARR: ~22%), avoiding 6.6% secondary surgical episodes. The economic breakeven threshold sits at exactly $42,424 USD, delivering positive value in comprehensive US private or complex salvage pathways (e.g., conversion to Reverse Shoulder Arthroplasty).
